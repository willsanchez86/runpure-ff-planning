# Hosting options — research (2026-11-19)

**Question:** where to host the three pieces — always-on Node backend (Express + pipeline, ADR-0008), MySQL (ADR-0005), Next.js frontend (ADR-0002) — under the owner's constraints (owner, 2026-11-19): ≤ $15/month, free tiers strongly preferred where the compromises are acceptable, one provider preferred, first deploy ASAP after the auth milestone.
**Workload facts:** ~410 MB in MySQL today (ETL spec §7.3); backend idles low (one user, one daily 06:00 ET ingest; the later live tier polls during game windows); the pipeline needs real MySQL behaviour — session `TEMPORARY` staging tables, foreign keys. The backend refuses to start in production with auth disabled, so the auth milestone gates public reachability.
**Earlier research:** [`mysql-hosting-options-research-2026-11-08.md`](mysql-hosting-options-research-2026-11-08.md) (MySQL-only; provider pricing below supersedes its numbers where they differ).

## 1. The owner's Heroku question — answered

Heroku has had **no free tier since November 2022**. Cheapest always-on dyno is **Basic, $7/month** (0.5 GB; Eco at $5 sleeps after 30 minutes — a sleeping dyno misses the 06:00 scheduler). MySQL is not first-party: the JawsDB add-on's free Kitefin plan is **5 MB** (our data is ~410 MB — unusable); the first usable plan is Leopard at **$10/month max**. Realistic Heroku total: $7 (backend) + $10 (MySQL) + frontend elsewhere ≈ **$17+/month** — over budget and two vendors anyway. **Viable, not competitive.** ([Heroku pricing overviews](https://computesphere.com/compare/heroku-pricing), [no-free-tier history](https://convika.com/compare/heroku-free-tier/), [JawsDB plans](https://elements.heroku.com/addons/jawsdb), [Heroku MySQL options 2026](https://kuberns.com/blogs/heroku-database-options/))

## 2. Railway — the clean one-provider path

- **Hobby plan $5/month, includes $5 of usage**; beyond that, actual usage bills: **$10/GB RAM/month, $20/vCPU/month (billed per second on real usage), $0.15/GB volume, $0.05/GB egress**. One-click managed MySQL as a service in the same project, private networking included. Docker or native builds both supported. ([railway.com/pricing](https://railway.com/pricing), [plan docs](https://dev.to/nayankyada/railway-pricing-2026-free-tier-limits-usage-costs-when-to-upgrade-1acm))
- **Estimate for us** (uncalibrated, from the rates above and our idle profile): MySQL ~0.4–0.5 GB resident ≈ $4–5 + backend ~0.2 GB ≈ $2 + near-zero CPU + ~1 GB volume ≈ $0.15 → **≈ $7–10/month all-in** after the included $5. Frontend on Railway would add ~$2–4; on Vercel's free Hobby tier it adds $0.
- No permanent free tier (one-time $5 trial).

## 3. Render and Fly — weaker fits

- **Render:** free web services **sleep after 15 minutes** and **cron is not free-eligible**; no managed MySQL (Postgres-centric) — MySQL means a paid private service + disk. Always-on backend starts at $7. Wrong shape for a MySQL app. ([Render free-tier limits](https://freetier.co/directory/products/render), [docs summary](https://whichdevtool.com/tools/render/))
- **Fly.io:** free allowances **removed October 2024**; pay-as-you-go machines from ~$2/month + $0.15/GB volumes; **no managed MySQL** (managed Postgres only, $38). Self-managed MySQL on a volume ≈ $5–8/month total — cheap but all ops on us, on a platform with no free cushion. ([Fly free-tier removal](https://www.saaspricepulse.com/blog/flyio-pricing-history), [current pricing](https://deploymanage.com/blog/fly-io-pricing))

## 4. The $0 path — Oracle Cloud Always Free + Vercel

- Oracle's Always Free Arm allowance was **halved without announcement in June 2026** to **2 OCPUs / 12 GB** (still the only true always-on $0 compute). Risks: **idle-reclaim** — instances under ~20% CPU/network/memory utilization over 7 days may be reclaimed (our idle profile is exactly that); **capacity** — the home region may have no Arm capacity when claiming; the June cut signals ongoing free-tier erosion. Everything (backend + MySQL in Docker) would run on one VM we fully operate: MySQL config, backups, TLS, OS updates, ARM images. ([2026 halving](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/oracle_always_free_tier_reduced_2026.html), [idle-reclaim rule](https://bestusavps.com/reviews/oracle-cloud/), [current allowance](https://www.epifive.com/blog/oracle-cloud-always-free-tier-what-you-get))
- Frontend would sit on **Vercel's Hobby tier** (free for personal, non-commercial use — our case; [vercel.com/pricing](https://vercel.com/pricing)).

## 5. Free managed MySQL-compatible: TiDB Cloud Starter

- **Aiven retired its free MySQL** for new services (cheapest is $5/month Developer; its free Postgres remains). ([aiven.io/free-mysql-database](https://aiven.io/free-mysql-database))
- **TiDB Cloud Starter** (ex-Serverless): always-free quota **5 GiB row storage + 50M Request Units per month per instance** — plenty for 410 MB and a daily ingest. **Compatibility verified for our needs:** foreign keys (v6.6+), temporary tables (local temp tables can't carry FKs — ours don't). Caveats: it is TiDB, not vanilla MySQL (minor SQL differences possible at the margins); **hitting the monthly quota refuses new connections until the next month** — a fail-closed outage. ([Starter pricing](https://www.pingcap.com/tidb-cloud-starter-pricing-details/), [locking functions](https://docs.pingcap.com/tidb/stable/locking-functions/), [FK docs](https://docs.pingcap.com/tidb/stable/foreign-key/), [temp tables](https://docs.pingcap.com/tidb/stable/temporary-tables/))
- Pairing: Railway backend (~$5) + TiDB free DB + Vercel free frontend ≈ **$5–7/month** with a managed DB — the cheapest managed-everything mix, at the cost of a second vendor and the TiDB caveats.

## 6. AWS — included at the owner's request (2026-11-19), excluded on the facts

AWS replaced its 12-month free tier for accounts created after July 15, 2025: a new account gets **$100 in credits (up to $200 with activities), and on the Free plan the account is suspended after six months or when credits run out — always-free services included**. There is no longer a path to run an always-on EC2/RDS stack free beyond ~6 months on a new account. Paid paths, broken out (verified 2026): Lightsail $5 instance (1 vCPU, 0.5 GB, 20 GB SSD) + Lightsail managed MySQL $15 (1 GB, 40 GB SSD) = **$20/month** for the managed-database path; or a single Lightsail instance with self-managed MySQL at **$5/month** (0.5 GB, tight; $10 for 2 GB) — the same self-ops burden the Oracle/GCP VMs carry for $0; or EC2 + self-managed ≈ $10–15 with more complexity. **Excluded: the "free tier" is a 6-month trial for our purposes; the managed path is over budget; the self-managed path is dominated by the free VMs (same work, $0) and by Railway (managed, similar money).** ([Lightsail pricing](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/aws-lightsail-pricing-plans-and-extra-costs-2026), [plan details](https://kuberns.com/blogs/post/aws-lightsail-pricing-your-comprehensive-guide/)) ([AWS free-plan changes](https://awsi.click/en/articles/aws-free-tier-comparison/), [2026 overview](https://builder.aws.com/content/3Kkt4kNiDSUnO3n6db2weSNubzE/the-dollar0-aws-account-in-2026-free-plan-credits-guardrails), [RDS free tier page](https://aws.amazon.com/rds/free/))

## 7. GCP e2-micro — the second true always-free VM

Google's Always Free tier still includes (verified November 2026) **one e2-micro VM** (2 shared vCPUs at a 25% duty cycle, **1 GB RAM**, 30 GB disk, 1 GB/month North-America egress, us-west1/us-central1/us-east1 only), with no expiry and — unlike Oracle — **no documented idle-reclaim policy**. The constraint is the 1 GB RAM: backend (~200 MB) + MySQL (~400–500 MB) + OS fits only with swap and tuned MySQL — workable for our size, permanently tight. Same full self-ops burden as the Oracle path. ([freetier.wiki](https://freetier.wiki/services/compute-engine-e2-micro/), [limits by product](https://www.cloudapicredits.com/guides/google-cloud-free-tier/))

## 8. Small-PaaS sweep (owner asked what else exists)

- **Koyeb** — free instance scales to zero after an hour *and* the free tier is closed to new users post-acquisition. Excluded. ([status](https://toolfreebie.com/koyeb-free-tier-alternatives/))
- **Zeabur** — a rolling $5/month usage credit; fitting backend + MySQL inside $5 is doubtful and the track record is thin. Not pursued. ([comparison](https://hostgage.com/best-free-nodejs-hosting/))
- **Northflank Sandbox** — **verified on northflank.com/pricing:** free plan with **2 services + 2 jobs + 1 database, up to $20/month of resources, "always on, no sleeping", MySQL among the managed datastores, no card, no stated time limit**. That is exactly our shape (backend service + MySQL database; the 2nd service could even host the frontend) at $0 on one provider. **UNVERIFIED until setup:** the resource granularity inside the $20 allowance (CPU/RAM per service), disk/egress limits, and backup behaviour on the free database. Young-platform risk: a free tier this generous can be cut, as Oracle's and Koyeb's were. ([northflank.com/pricing](https://northflank.com/pricing))
- **Vercel as the full host (owner asked 2026-11-19; specifics verified)** — first-party for the Next.js frontend, free on Hobby for personal non-commercial use; in every shape for that role. For the backend the verified limits are: **no persistent processes** — all compute is functions, Hobby max duration **300 s** (Pro 800 s, 30 min in beta), so a long ingest or any always-on server is out at every tier; **Hobby crons run at most once per day and fire anywhere within the scheduled hour** — the 06:00 ingest gets loose timing and the future 60-second live poller is impossible (Pro at $20/month allows frequent crons, busting the budget before a database exists). Hosting the backend here would mean rewriting it as chunked short-lived functions — reversing ADR-0008. **Databases, corrected:** Vercel hosts none natively; the Marketplace provisions partners — Postgres (Neon, Supabase, Prisma) and Redis (Upstash), plus one MySQL-compatible option, **PlanetScale** — which has no free tier (dropped 2024) and whose entry pricing is over budget, with Vitess-margin semantics we'd have to verify. So: a MySQL path technically exists on the Marketplace but not within constraints. **Role: frontend only.** ([function limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations), [30-min functions changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-functions-can-now-run-up-to-30-minutes), [Hobby cron limits](https://steadycron.com/guides/vercel-cron-limits/), [cron docs](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/manage-cron-jobs), [Marketplace storage](https://vercel.com/docs/marketplace-storage))
- Not explored (unchanged reasons): plain VPS (Hetzner/DO/Linode — same ops as a free VM but not free), PlanetScale (free tier dropped 2024), Supabase/Neon (Postgres), Cloudflare Workers/D1 (not Node/MySQL), home self-hosting (uptime tied to the house).

## 9. Shapes to decide between

| | Shape | Est. monthly | Compromises |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | **Railway** (backend + MySQL) + **Vercel** frontend (free) | ~$7–10 | Two consoles (one bill that matters); no free compute |
| B | **Railway everything** | ~$9–13 | Single console; pays for what Vercel gives free |
| C | **Oracle Always Free VM** (Docker: backend + MySQL) + **Vercel** (free) | ~$0 | Full self-ops (MySQL, backups, TLS, updates, ARM); idle-reclaim and capacity risk; free-tier erosion trend |
| C2 | **GCP e2-micro VM** (Docker: backend + MySQL) + **Vercel** (free) | ~$0 | Same self-ops; 1 GB RAM is permanently tight (swap + tuned MySQL); no idle-reclaim policy, steadier than Oracle |
| D | Railway backend + **TiDB Starter** (free DB) + Vercel (free) | ~$5–7 | Third vendor; TiDB-not-MySQL margins; quota exhaustion = fail-closed outage |
| E | **Northflank Sandbox** (backend + MySQL, optionally frontend) + Vercel (free) | ~$0 | Managed and always-on at $0 — but resource granularity/disk/egress/backups unverified until setup, and a generous free tier can be cut (Oracle and Koyeb precedents) |
| — | Heroku (Basic + JawsDB) | ~$17+ | Over budget; ruled out (§1) |
| — | AWS | 6-month trial or $20+ | Excluded on the facts (§6) |

All estimates are **uncalibrated** until a first month of real billing. Every option keeps the deploy gates: MTDP first, auth before public reachability, MySQL over TLS, backups per ETL spec D16 (on C, backups are entirely ours to build).
